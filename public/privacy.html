MOFO extension and service · 25 September 2026

MOFO is a Chrome extension for fomo.family. This page says what it reads, what leaves your browser and what doesn't.

What stays in your browser

  • The chart analysis, the entry check, the token risk checks, your rules and the drawings on the chart are computed in your browser.
  • MOFO reads the fomo.family page you have open (the chart, the token stats, your open position and closed trades) to do that. It never clicks, trades or signs anything, and it has no access to your wallet or your fomo.family login.
  • Your settings, your trading notes, your trade journal and the holder log are stored in the extension's own storage in this browser.

What goes to the MOFO service

Only when you use a feature that needs it, and only what that feature needs:

  • Narrative rundown: the token's chain, contract address, name, ticker, its links and age.
  • Chat: your question, the recent messages of that chat, and a snapshot of the token on screen (chart levels, candles, flow, holders, your position and rules, and your trading notes if you wrote any).
  • Holder profiles and trader cards: the token address or the wallet address being looked up.
  • A random install id, created when you install MOFO, so the daily free limits work. It isn't linked to your name, wallet or accounts.

The service passes these to Anthropic (Claude, which writes the rundown and the chat answers), twitterapi.io (searches public posts on X) and GMGN (public on-chain holder and wallet data). Rundowns and holder lookups are cached for up to 30 minutes so the same token isn't looked up twice. Daily usage counters are kept for three days. MOFO doesn't sell data, show ads or track you across sites.

Your own keys

If you add your own API keys in the settings, MOFO calls Claude, twitterapi.io and GMGN directly with them and the MOFO service isn't used. The keys stay in this browser's extension storage; the fomo.family page can't read them.

Other requests

MOFO reads a small settings file from mindoverfomo.tools to find the service, and asks GeckoTerminal for public price history when grading your journal.
